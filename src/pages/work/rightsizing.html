LLM-assisted AWS rightsizing

A tool that reads utilisation across 1,300+ AWS resources, asks an LLM which ones are idle or oversized, and sends the list to the people who own them.

  • $32K→$29.5Kmonthly AWS spend
  • ~$30Ksaved a year, about 8%
  • 1,300+resources: 400+ EC2, 800+ EFS, ~160 RDS

The problem

The estate was large: hundreds of EC2 instances, two or three EFS volumes per instance, and around 160 RDS databases. Checking each one by hand for waste does not scale, and the person looking at a metric is rarely the person who knows whether the workload still matters.

What was built

  • An export of CPU and memory utilisation for EC2, EFS and RDS.
  • An LLM review that labels each resource as idle, underutilised or over-provisioned.
  • Email reports to resource owners, so the call to stop, shrink or keep stays with the people who know the workload.
  • Alongside it, a nightly scheduler that stops non-production instances.

Result

Monthly AWS spend went from $32,000 to $29,500, about $30K a year. That figure comes from the rightsizing work; the scheduler is on top of it.
